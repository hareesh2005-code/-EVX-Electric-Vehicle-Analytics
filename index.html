    What EVX calculates

    Driving range
    Usable energy above your reserve divided by energy use per kilometre, with a spread across speed, load and gradient.
    Trip energy
    Energy for a planned distance, the battery level at arrival, and the margin left, including climbing.
    Charging
    Energy, time and an SOC-versus-time curve, with an optional slowdown at high charge levels.
    Running cost
    Cost per charge, per kilometre and per month from your own tariff and currency.
    Road load
    Rolling resistance, aerodynamic drag, gradient and acceleration forces turned into wheel power.
    Motor and drivetrain
    A loss-based motor model whose efficiency changes with torque and speed, plus drivetrain and controller losses.
    Battery
    Internal resistance, voltage sag under load, I²R loss and SOC tracking by coulomb counting.
    Drive cycles
    Time-stepped speed, power, current and SOC for built-in or pasted speed profiles.
    Sensitivity
    Which parameters move energy use the most, shown as a ranked chart.
    Export
    CSV files for speed sweeps and drive cycles, a JSON configuration file and a print-ready report.

    How one number is produced

    Energy use is worked out backwards from the road to the battery, so every loss is accounted for.

    1. Road loadRolling, drag and gradient forces at the wheel
    2. DrivetrainGear and transmission losses
    3. MotorCopper, iron and friction losses
    4. ControllerInverter switching and conduction losses
    5. BatteryInternal resistance, voltage sag and current

    What the estimates are, and are not

    What they are

    Simplified engineering-model estimates that respond correctly to speed, mass, gradient, efficiency and battery changes. Each output is labelled with its model level.

    What they are not

    They are not validated against measured vehicles and they are not a guarantee of real-world range. EVX is not a battery management or safety system and does not connect to a vehicle.

    How to get better results

    Replace the sample values with data for your own vehicle. The Model page lists every equation, assumption and limit so you can judge fit for your case.

        Calculation methodology

        1. Read the vehicle configuration and the operating point (speed, gradient, state of charge).
        2. Convert to SI units and compute the road-load forces and wheel power.
        3. Work backwards through drivetrain, motor and controller to the battery terminal power, adding the auxiliary load.
        4. Solve the battery equation for current and terminal voltage, which gives the battery loss.
        5. Divide chemical power by speed for energy use, and usable energy by energy use for range.
        6. For a drive cycle, repeat steps 2 to 4 every second and integrate current to track SOC.

        Road load

        At speed v, total tractive force is the sum of four terms, and wheel power is force times speed.

        F_roll   = Crr · m · g · cos θ
F_aero   = ½ · ρ · Cd · A · v²
F_grade  = m · g · sin θ              θ = atan(gradient / 100)
F_inert  = m · k · a                  k = rotating mass factor
P_wheel  = (F_roll + F_aero + F_grade + F_inert) · v

        Powertrain

        P_shaft    = P_wheel / eta_drivetrain
P_motor_in = P_shaft + P_loss(T, w)          (loss model)
P_terminal = P_motor_in / eta_controller + P_aux
w = v / r_wheel * gear_ratio                 (motor speed)

        The motor loss model splits losses into copper (proportional to torque squared), iron (proportional to speed to the power 1.5) and friction (proportional to speed). Coefficients are set so the total loss at the rated point equals P_rated x (1/eta_rated - 1), divided . A flat-efficiency option is also available. The model assumes the motor can hold the commanded torque; field weakening is not modelled in detail, and power available is limited to the peak rating, reduced in proportion to speed below base speed.

        Battery

        V_terminal = E - I * R
P_terminal = V_terminal * I
I = (E - sqrt(E^2 - 4 R P_terminal)) / (2 R)
P_chemical = E * I = P_terminal + I^2 * R
dSOC = - I * dt / capacity                   (coulomb counting)

        E is the nominal voltage and is held constant. Real packs show a voltage that changes with charge level, temperature and age, none of which is modelled. Negative P_terminal represents regeneration.

        Range, trip, charging and cost

        Usable energy = (SOC - reserve)/100 * V * Ah
Range         = usable energy / (Wh per km)
Trip climb    = m * g * height / chain_efficiency
Charging      : battery power = charger power * efficiency (linear taper above 80% if enabled)
Cost per km   = (Wh per km / 1000) / charging_efficiency * tariff

        Assumptions you should know about

        • Sample profiles are generic and do not describe any real manufacturer's vehicle.
        • Constant battery EMF, constant drivetrain and controller efficiency, no temperature effects.
        • No wind, no tyre pressure effects, no battery ageing. State of health is not estimated.
        • Steady-speed results ignore acceleration; the drive-cycle includes it.
        • Regeneration is optional and depends on the share, power limit and efficiencies you set.
        • The trip estimator credits no energy for descents.
        • Range scenarios use fixed definitions: low, moderate and higher speed are 30%, 50% and 70% of the modelled top speed; light load is no payload; heavy load is the sample's maximum payload; uphill is a 3% gradient.
        • Built-in drive profiles are illustrative and are not WLTP, Indian Driving Cycle or any other standardized test.

        Estimated, simulated and measured values

        KindMeaning in EVX
        InputA value you enter or configure.
        Model assumptionA value held fixed in the configuration because measured data is not available, such as drag coefficient or drivetrain efficiency.
        CalculatedA value produced by the model from the inputs and assumptions. It is an estimate or simulation result, not a measurement.
        MeasuredEVX does not import or display measured vehicle data in this version.

        Limitations

        EVX is an estimation and learning tool. It is not a battery management system, not a safety monitor and not certified for any regulatory use. It does not read data from a vehicle, so nothing in it is live telemetry. Actual range depends on vehicle design, weather, road, load, battery condition, driving style and control software.
